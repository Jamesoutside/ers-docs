👥 Staff & People
Staff & People
How NH describes the people they need to house. Use this language when describing who ERS serves.
Word / Phrase What It Means & How to Use It
Itinerant staff Steve's umbrella term for non-permanent staff — locums, agency, travel nurses. 'Itinerant staff in the north interior will continue to be important.'
Travel employees Go Health BC nurses — NH employees who travel between sites. 'We get to deploy them.'
Locums Rotating physicians on short-term contracts. Managed by Medical Affairs/Medaffairs.
Agency nurses Private agency staff (not Go Health) — historically paid a flat fee, sometimes with their own housing arrangements.
Go Health nurses NH's own travel nurses — trained in NH systems, deployed flexibly. ~600 in the program.
Permanent people Long-term NH employees. Steve: 'recruiting permanent people and retaining them — housing's an issue.'
Clinical staff Broad term for all healthcare workers: nurses, lab, diagnostics, imaging.
Lab staff / lab techs Lab technicians — one of the staff groups needing housing.
Diagnostics — X-Ray, MRI, CT Diagnostic imaging staff — another group in the housing mix.
Specialists Specialist physicians — longer transitional stays, higher-end housing needs.
Physicians Doctors managed through Medical Affairs. Locum housing coming into the fold.
Deploy Steve's verb for assigning staff. 'Deploy a nurse.' 'Deploy them to rooms.' Use this — it's their operational language.
Convert them into full time Steve's goal for Go Health nurses: good experience → permanent hire. This is the retention outcome ERS enables.

🔴 Problem Language
Problem Language
Steve's own words for what's broken. Use these to mirror his reality in conversations and decks.
Word / Phrase What It Means & How to Use It
Dog's breakfast Steve's description of the current booking/leasing situation. Chaotic, inconsistent, uncoordinated.
Tripping over ourselves Multiple groups booking the same spaces with no coordination.
150 leases Approximate current portfolio size. Steve: 'I'd rather not have 150 leases.'
Different standards Different quality thresholds between Medical Affairs, Operations, and Go Health — causing low utilization.
We're cleaning, we're putting toilet paper on rolls — that's not our core business Steve's exact quote. The most powerful mirror-back line in any ERS conversation.
Utilization wasn't ideal Beds sitting empty because of booking fragmentation and standard mismatches.
Peaks and valleys Demand volatility — surge times vs. quiet periods. NH currently leases to the peak and pays for empty beds.
Surge times Holiday periods, Christmas — when demand spikes above average.
Empty 90% of the time What happens when NH leases to peak demand — most beds empty most of the time.
Double paying Paying for a leased unit AND an Airbnb when a nurse needs a pet-friendly room. A real example Steve gave.
Spiking Agency use spiking during the pandemic — what triggered the housing crisis.
Massively fluid right now Steve's description of the current portfolio state — everything in transition.
Right sizing Getting supply to match actual demand — neither too much nor too little.
Administrative cost The overhead NH carries managing housing internally. Steve wants to remove this into a single service cost.
No pets A specific example of inflexibility causing double-paying. Housing rules forcing workarounds.
Not our core business Steve's framing. Repeat this back — 'and we agree — it shouldn't be.'
✅ Solution Language
Solution Language
How Steve describes what he's looking for. Every ERS pitch should use these exact phrases.
Word / Phrase What It Means & How to Use It
One stop shop Steve's top priority — one company, multiple properties, multiple cities, all handled. Said it three times.
Get out of that business Steve's goal. 'I don't want to be in that business.' ERS lets NH exit the housing business entirely.
Blended strategy Steve's vision — a mix of owned, partner-operated, and leased housing. ERS is the operator in this blend.
Value proposition What any partner must demonstrate. Steve: 'a value proposition that makes sense — that's what I'm looking for.'
Consistent, stable accommodations at a rate that can drive some efficiency Steve's exact description of the ideal outcome. Quote this back verbatim.
Walkable, quality, safe Steve's three non-negotiables for any housing. Use this as ERS's standard descriptor.
Convert them into full time The Go Health goal. Good housing experience → permanent hire. ERS enables this conversion.
Experience matters Steve: 'experience does matter.' ERS's entire model is built on this premise.
Max our utilization Filling beds efficiently — the financial driver behind getting a single operator.
Leveling out the peaks and valleys Managing demand variability — a single operator with multiple properties can do this; NH can't alone.
Find that sweet spot The balance between leasing to peak vs. under-capacity. ERS's utilization management does this.
Per head in the bed Steve's cost metric — what NH pays per occupied night. ERS's flat-rate model speaks this language.
Single cost as part of a service The billing model Steve wants: cleaning, booking, management — all in one number.
Five year terms / longer term commitments What Steve wants to move toward once data is ready. ERS should position for 5-year arrangements.
Purpose builds New construction designed for healthcare housing. DC Hub is ERS's answer.
Multipurpose use of this type of space Flexibility to serve corporate travel, locums, students, potentially patients — without compromising each group.
Nobody in Northern BC doing that kind of thing Steve's gap statement. This is ERS's market position — said directly by the customer.
