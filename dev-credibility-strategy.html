Development Credibility & Capital Strategy

Internal Brief · Austin, Siavash, Lori · June 2, 2026

The Problem We're Solving
Northern Health is moving toward us. The next question they — and every lender — will ask is: can you actually build this?
Tom Kenny and BOS Architecture answer the design question. We need someone who answers the development and capital question. A name that shows up in a search, on a reference call, or across a table from a lender — and carries weight.
The gap

We have the operator, the architect, the government support, the community alignment, and the healthcare authority moving our way. What we don't have visibly on the team is a senior development figure with a completed track record who also has the means and connections to help secure construction financing. That's the gap. One person — or one entity — closes it.

What We're Looking For
Two ways someone can come in. They don't need to cover everything — but ideally they cover both.
The right partner brings either investment capital, development credibility, or ideally both. We're not asking for everything — but the more they bring, the better the position for everyone.
Option A
The Credibility & Development Partner
Senior name — recognizable, searchable, respected in BC real estate or healthcare development
Completed project track record — buildings delivered, not just planned
Co-developer on record — gives NH and lenders confidence we can execute
Lender relationships — opens doors we haven't opened yet
Development fee or modest equity as compensation
Option B — Preferred
The Cornerstone Equity Co-Developer
Invests equity in the project — skin in the game, aligned incentives
Equity position secures lender confidence — their balance sheet backs the loan
Co-develops on record — delivers the credibility answer to NH and PHSA
Helps secure construction mortgage through their connections and standing
One project scope — DC Hub — with eyes open on the provincial opportunity
The key distinction

Option B isn't just credibility — it's a real equity raise. They invest in and co-develop the DC Hub. Their equity is their security. Their name is our credibility. Their connections help us secure the loan. ERS keeps the management company, the operating model, and the full pipeline — the development partner gets their return on this project and first right of conversation on what comes next.

Who We're Looking For
Senior. Recognized. Has the means. Not looking to replicate.
The right person is someone whose name closes the room — not someone who opens a competing company once they understand the model.
Best fit
Senior BC Developer — Individual (Retired or Semi-Retired)
20–40 year track record. Name that appears on a reference call. Deep lender and municipal relationships. Looking for one strong final project — not a new business. No corporate machine behind them. Low threat of replication. Could be introduced through Tom Kenny, John Dyak, or Rob Johnson's networks.
Strong fit
Family Office with Real Estate & Development Arm
Quiet capital, no press ambitions. Sees healthcare housing as a differentiated asset class with government-backed demand. Has the balance sheet to co-invest and the relationships to help at the lender level. Wants a niche play, not a national expansion. Approached privately — not through public channels.
Strategic fit
Indigenous Development Corporation
Band-owned or Métis development entities with completed projects and balance sheet. Adds reconciliation credibility with NH, government funders, and PHSA simultaneously. Taltan First Nation expressed interest in DC-region partnership — explore whether they have a development arm or connection to one. Extremely difficult to compete against ERS once they're inside the tent.
Worth exploring
Healthcare / Impact Real Estate Fund Manager
Niche funds that back healthcare-adjacent or community housing development. Not trying to operate — looking for differentiated assets with social return. Brings investor credibility, possible co-investment, and sector-specific validation. Ask Siavash — Impact Canada connections may open this door.
Avoid
National Builders · Anyone in Healthcare Housing · Anyone Wanting Operational Control
Ledcor, PCL, Bosa, Concert — too much infrastructure to replicate once they see the model. Anyone already in workforce or healthcare housing is a direct competitive threat regardless of NDA. Anyone pushing for operational involvement post-construction is trying to become ERS.
